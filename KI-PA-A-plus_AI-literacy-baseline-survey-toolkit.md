# AI literacy baseline survey toolkit

Power Academy | AI Learning Studio · KI-PA-A+ · Version 1 (draft, 25 October 2026)

Used in **B10** (Article 4 measures per role), **B23** (pilot plan, step 0) and **B24** (30/60/90 follow-up). Analysis template: *KI-PA-A-plus_AI-literacy-baseline_analysis-template.xlsx* (Resources).

> **What this is:** a planning instrument to find out which roles need which AI-literacy measures. **What it is not:** a validated competence test, a performance assessment, a course grade or proof of compliance. Before you use it with real colleagues, clarify data protection, co-determination (e.g. works council) and run a pilot. In the course, use synthetic data only.

**Guiding rule:** a skill gap, an organisational blocker and "I can't judge" are three different findings. Unclear rules are not fixed by training.

---

## 1 · Survey structure

| Module | Who | Content |
|---|---|---|
| A · Tasks and use | everyone | area, tasks, touchpoints with AI, frequency, tools, approval status |
| B · Individual confidence | everyone | 10 self-ratings + 3 situations |
| C · Attitude and learning needs | everyone | benefit, concerns, blockers, learning needs, formats, time |
| D · Leadership and implementation | people leaders only | leadership tasks, implementation status, team readiness, support needed |

**Introduction (template):** "We want to understand where AI already supports your work, which questions are open and which learning offers would help. No prior knowledge is needed; this is not an assessment of your performance. By AI we mean e.g. chatbots, writing and research assistants, analysis tools and AI features in existing software. Please do not enter names or confidential information in text fields."

Add before launch: responsible unit, purpose, voluntary participation, who sees what, retention period, privacy notice. Promise "anonymous" only if collection and analysis really ensure it (section 4).

## 2 · Questions and scales

### Module A · Tasks and use

| ID | Question | Format |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | Main area of work | single choice, broad categories + "prefer not to say" |
| A2 | Tasks that shape your working day | multiple choice, max. 4 |
| A3 | Your touchpoints with AI | multiple choice: use AI myself · work with AI outputs · decide on introduction · develop/operate AI · review AI use · lead people whose work changes · none (exclusive) · don't know (exclusive) |
| A4 | Do you have leadership responsibility? | disciplinary · functional · both · no → if yes: module D |
| A5 | How often did you use AI for work in the last four weeks? | 0 not at all · 1 on one day · 2 several days, less than weekly · 3 once or twice a week · 4 three to four times a week · 5 (almost) daily · "don't know whether my tools contain AI" |
| A6 | Which AI tools or features do you use? | optional text; tool names only |
| A7 | What do you use AI for? (users only) | multiple choice |
| A8 | Approval status of the tools you use (users only) | all approved for my purpose · only partly known · don't know where to check · not all approved · prefer not to say |
| A9 | Which task could AI help you with most? | optional text |

### Module B · Individual confidence

**B1 · How independently can you do the following today?**
1 not yet · 2 with step-by-step guidance · 3 partly on my own · 4 on my own for usual tasks · 5 on my own also for new or demanding tasks · DK can't judge · NR not relevant for my tasks

| ID | Statement |
|---|---|
| B1.1 | I can explain what my AI tools do and where their limits are. |
| B1.2 | I can decide whether a task is suitable for AI support. |
| B1.3 | I can describe task, context and expected result clearly. |
| B1.4 | I can improve an inadequate result with targeted changes. |
| B1.5 | I can check key statements against suitable sources or expert criteria. |
| B1.6 | I can judge when I need expert support or an approval. |
| B1.7 | Based on our rules, I can decide which information I may enter. |
| B1.8 | I can check whether a tool is approved for my use. |
| B1.9 | I can recognise one-sided or discriminatory statements in AI outputs. |
| B1.10 | I can integrate AI into a workflow and plan the checks it needs. |

**B2 · Situations** (a conversation starter, not a test)

- **S1** An AI gives a convincing answer with sources for an important decision. What do you do first? a) use it, sources are given · b) check key statements and whether the sources support them · c) ask the same AI to confirm · d) don't know
- **S2** You want an AI tool to summarise a document with confidential customer information. What do you do first? a) upload it, it's internal · b) remove the customer name, that's enough · c) check whether tool, processing and purpose are permitted for this information · d) don't know where to check
- **S3** An AI recommendation seems to disadvantage certain groups of people. a) follow it, AI is objective · b) check basis and criteria for possible disadvantage, get support if needed · c) rephrase until the result looks plausible · d) unsure

### Module C · Attitude, blockers, learning needs

- **C1 Agreement** (1 strongly disagree … 5 strongly agree · DK): I see concrete ways AI supports my work · I want to try suitable applications · I can openly raise uncertainty · I have working time to learn · I worry AI will change my role for the worse *(report separately)*
- **C2 Blockers** (0 not at all · 1 slightly · 2 moderately · 3 strongly · 4 very strongly · DK): no access to approved tools · unclear rules or responsibilities · uncertainty about data protection · hard to judge result quality · no examples from my area · no time to learn · no expert support · insufficient quality of available tools
- **C3 Learning need** (0 none · 1 low · 2 medium · 3 high · 4 very high · DK · NR): basics and limits · use cases for my job · prompts · checking results · data protection and safe use · internal rules and contacts · fairness and responsibility · improving or automating workflows · technical development and integration. Then: "Which three first?"
- **C4 Formats** (1 not helpful … 5 very helpful · DK): short self-study · guided exercises with approved tools · workshops on own tasks · checklists and templates · peer exchange · office hours · coached practice project
- **C5 Time**, **C6 Preconditions** (planned working time, tool access, examples, manager support, safe space to try, accessible materials, clear rules and contacts), **C7/C8** open text

### Module D · Leadership (people leaders only)

- **D1 Leadership tasks** (scale as B1): identify use cases with the team · prioritise by benefit, effort, risk · set criteria to test benefit · clarify who checks and who decides · know when to involve data protection, IT security, legal · determine competence needs per task · discuss concerns and task changes · design workflows with matching controls · check whether training changes practice
- **D2 Implementation status** (0 not addressed · 1 need recognised · 2 planned · 3 partly implemented · 4 implemented · DK · NR): use cases recorded · tools and purposes known · approvals and rules clear · responsibilities named · review requirements set · role-based learning needs collected · learning time planned · reporting route known · quality and benefit criteria set
- **D3 Team readiness** (1 not at all … 5 very well · "not observed" · NR) + "What is your rating based on?"
- **D4 Support needed** (max. 3) · **D5** one concrete change in three months and how you would recognise progress

## 3 · How to analyse

1. **Code cleanly.** Scale values as numbers; `DK`, `NR` as text; blank = no answer or not shown. Never store these as 0.
2. **State the basis first.** Invited, started, completed, valid answers per item, share of leaders. Write "among respondents", not "all employees".
3. **Use one key figure per block** with its own denominator:

| Block | Key figure | Denominator |
|---|---|---|
| B1, D1 | share 1–2 ("support needed") | answers 1–5 only; DK/NR reported separately |
| S1–S3 | share of intended answer (S1 b, S2 c, S3 b) | all answers |
| C1 | share 4–5 per statement | answers 1–5 |
| C2, C3 | share 3–4 | answers 0–4 |
| C4 | share 4–5 | answers 1–5 |
| D2 | share 0–2 ("not yet in place") | answers 0–4 |

4. **No overall score**, no averages on their own, no cut-offs such as "3.5 = competent".
5. **Combine self-rating and situation** as a learning hypothesis (S1↔B1.5, S2↔B1.7, S3↔B1.9): confident + other answer → discuss the case and rules; unsure + intended answer → practise to build confidence.
6. **Compare groups** on specific items (leaders vs. non-leaders, regular vs. occasional users) — never rank departments.
7. **Open text:** remove identifying details, code by theme (use case, skill need, organisational blocker, support wish, concern, success criterion), count people per theme. "Frequent" and "critical" are different.

## 4 · Confidentiality checklist

- [ ] No names, staff IDs or e-mail addresses in the response data
- [ ] Only role attributes you need for measures; broad categories
- [ ] Tool metadata checked (account, IP address, timestamps, personal invitation links)
- [ ] Workshop sign-up collected separately, not linked to answers
- [ ] Minimum group size set — suggestion: 5 for rated items, 10 for comments (oriented on Microsoft Viva Glint defaults; not a legal threshold)
- [ ] Complementary suppression: if one group is hidden, prevent reconstruction by subtraction (hide the next smallest group or merge)
- [ ] Open text checked for identifying content before it is shared
- [ ] Access defined: analysis unit (raw data) · managers (protected aggregates) · leadership (organisation-wide) · training provider (learning needs only)
- [ ] Retention and deletion defined
- [ ] Privacy notice, legal basis and co-determination checked by the responsible units
- [ ] Later learning records kept separate from a survey announced as anonymous

Removing names is generally not enough to make data anonymous; combinations of attributes can identify people. Pseudonymised data remain personal data.

## 5 · From findings to measures

| Finding | Type of measure |
|---|---|
| "I can't do it yet." | learn and practise |
| "I don't know what is allowed." | clarify and communicate rules |
| "I have no suitable tool." | provide tools (IT/organisation) |
| "I have no time to learn." | work organisation, leadership |
| "I worry about my role." | dialogue, change support |
| "I can't judge my need." | orientation, diagnostic practice task |

**Learning objective template:** "After the measure, [target group] can [observable action] in [typical situation] while meeting [quality or safety criteria]."

**Prioritise by** 1) consequences of errors, 2) task relevance and number of people, 3) whether rules, tools and responsibilities are already clear (if not, clarify first).

**Check impact:** before (baseline), directly after (defined practice task), after 4–8 weeks (used at work? what still blocks?) — this fits your 30/60/90 plan from B24.
